# Deepshaft

**A first-person descent into a mine that is closing behind you.**

🎮 Play: https://deepshaft.benrichardson.dev

## What it is

Deepshaft is a first-person raycast crawl through a mine that is cut fresh from a
seed every time you go down. You take the cage to the next floor, and the next,
and the mine gets worse the whole way: more things, harder things, and eventually
a clock that starts sending them after you whether you have found the way down or
not.

**You never aim.** The gun engages whatever is awake in front of it, so the whole
game is *where you are looking, what you are spending, and how loud you just were*.
That is what makes it work on one thumb — and it is why sneaking matters, because
a gun that only shoots what has already noticed you means walking quietly is a real
option and every trigger pull is a decision to be heard.

Underneath it is a handful of rules that do not know about each other, which is
where everything interesting comes from. Firedamp is gas that anything can set off
and that hurts everybody equally, so a corridor stops being somewhere you retreat
down and becomes somewhere you set. A **grate** stops bodies but not bullets and
not eyes, so a drift behind one is a shooting gallery — and the chase AI, which
only searches the walkable graph, breaks off and comes the long way round, which
reads as flanking although nothing anywhere says "flank". A **sump** is too deep
for a Hauler and fine for everything else, so a mixed pack splits itself around
water and arrives in two waves nobody scripted. And a locked door quietly hands the
far half of the level a route to you the moment you open it, because the enemy flow
field is rebuilt over the current door state.

## How to play

**On a phone.** Put a thumb anywhere on the lower half of the screen. Up and down
walks; left and right turns. A gentle push turns slowly for lining up a shot, a
hard push spins you round. **USE** appears only when there is something to use — a
door, the cage, a mate on the floor. A second finger anywhere is an optional
look-drag, and you never need it.

**On a keyboard.** `W`/`S` walk, `A`/`D` strafe, `Q` and the arrows turn, or move
the mouse. `E` uses, `Tab` or `1`–`3` changes weapon, `Esc` pauses.

**The goal** depends on the run shape you pick:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Stope** | The open workings. Endless — your score is the deepest floor you stood on. |
| **Adit** | Five tight floors with a bottom you can actually reach. The one to learn on. |
| **Lode** | Ten floors, and the mine gives you *nothing*. No ammo, no carbide on the ground: kills are your only supply, and there are fewer, harder things to kill. |

An interactive tutorial runs the first time you open the game — seven steps on a
real floor, driving the real rules — and is replayable from **How to play**.

## Multiplayer

**Live peer-to-peer co-op for two to four**, against the mine rather than each
other. Create a room and send the link, scan the QR, or type the four-character
code. A player at zero HP goes *down* rather than dying, and a mate has
twenty-two seconds to reach them; the run ends when everybody is down.

It is host-authoritative at 20Hz, and the map is **never sent** — a floor is a pure
function of `(seed, floor, mode, players)`, so every browser cuts a byte-identical
mine from one byte on the wire. That is also what makes host transfer survivable: a
promoted peer already holds the geometry and only needs the last snapshot to know
where everything was standing.

The reason 20Hz feels fine is a game decision rather than a transport one: no enemy
is a hitscanner, and every attack has a visible, audible wind-up of 0.42–0.66
seconds. A guest sees enemies about a tenth of a second in the past, and a tell four
times longer than that is a tell you still have time to answer.

There is no server. Your browsers talk to each other directly over WebRTC; a public
signalling relay introduces them once and then carries nothing.

## Tech

- Vite 6 + vanilla TypeScript
- **Canvas 2D raycaster** — column DDA into a single `Uint32Array`, one
  `putImageData` per frame, a ~170-column internal buffer stretched by the
  compositor, and fog as a lookup table rather than a multiply
- Procedural everything: wall textures, sprites, icons and sounds are generated by
  code at boot. No images, no fonts, no samples.
- Shared engine: fixed-timestep loop, floating analog thumbstick, deterministic
  RNG, procedural audio, Trystero P2P netcode
- Vitest — 392 tests across 16 files, including a headless balance simulation, a
  zero-tolerance mechanism audit, a contrast gate and a tutorial replay
- GitHub Pages hosting

No cookies, no fingerprinting, no third-party fonts. Anonymous, cookie-less
page-view counts via Cloudflare Web Analytics.
